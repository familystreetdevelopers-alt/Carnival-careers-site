Consumer rights.
Evidence first.

When a household gets unwanted marketing calls, the response is documented, accurate and jurisdiction-specific. We help people recognize a possible violation, preserve evidence, use official complaints and obtain independent legal review where appropriate.

Not a settlement guarantee. A call does not automatically create a payable invoice. Canadian regulator fines are not automatically paid to callers. In the U.S., a TCPA recovery depends on the specific facts, legal requirements and available remedies. CC does not impersonate lawyers or initiate litigation on a family's behalf.

Choose the law that applies

The number's country and the calling circumstances matter; cross-border cases may need specialist advice.

Canada — CRTC National Do Not Call List

Register an eligible residential or wireless number on the National DNCL. New registrations are added quickly, but telemarketers have a 31-day grace period to update their lists. Exemptions can apply, and business-to-business calls are treated differently.

Save the date, exact time, called number, displayed caller ID, company name if known, and what the caller said. Report a suspected breach through the CRTC's complaint guidance.

Financial truth: CRTC administrative penalties belong to the regulatory enforcement process. Do not enter a C$1,500 or C$15,000 fine as household or CC income. Any separate private claim requires its own legal basis and review.

United States — FTC registry / TCPA screening

Register an eligible personal number at DoNotCall.gov, allowing up to 31 days for covered calls to stop. Identify whether the call was a live sales call, prerecorded marketing call, or another type, and whether consent or an exemption applies.

For some TCPA violations, statutory damages can be US$500 per violation and may be increased by a court to no more than US$1,500 for knowing or willful conduct. A private claim under the Do Not Call subsection generally requires more than one qualifying call within 12 months by or on behalf of the same entity; other TCPA claims have different elements. Read 47 U.S.C. §227.

Financial truth: No assumed US$2,000–$3,000 NDA payment and no automatic payment for a single call. Report suspected violations at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, and have an appropriately licensed lawyer review potential private litigation or demand letters.

CC case-handling workflow

01 · Protect

Register + opt out

Use the appropriate national registry and request placement on a company's internal do-not-call list when appropriate.

02 · Capture

Keep real evidence

Record the facts of calls already received. Never fabricate contact, provoke calls, provide sensitive information or rely on spoofed caller ID alone.

03 · Review

Screen the rules

Check timing, country, consent, exemptions, company identity and whether repeated calls are by the same entity.

04 · Report

Use official channels

Submit a truthful complaint with preserved evidence. Do not threaten a company with baseless legal demands.

05 · Refer

Qualified legal review

Only counsel or the individual rights-holder decides whether a private claim or carefully supported demand makes sense.

06 · Account

Money only when real

Record no forecast settlement. Track expenses and confirmed funds separately, with proceeds belonging to the lawful recipient.

Evidence checklist

□ Called number and country (store securely)
□ Registry registration date / proof
□ Call date, time and timezone
□ Caller ID plus any identified business
□ Sales purpose, recorded message or live caller
□ Consent / business relationship facts
□ Opt-out requests and later calls
□ Screenshots, voicemail or lawful recordings
□ Complaint reference and status
□ Counsel review and actual disposition

Only save recordings when lawful. Caller ID may be spoofed. Keep phone numbers and case evidence out of public forms and shared marketing spreadsheets.
