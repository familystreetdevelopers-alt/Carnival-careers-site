Four practical tactics

Stop chasing the clock.
Track the actual fare.

01 · BEFORE BOOKING

Flexible travel days beat purchase-day rituals

Compare Tuesday/Wednesday travel against weekends, alternate airports, one-stop routes and baggage-inclusive all-in prices. The day you click “buy” is not a guaranteed advantage.

02 · PRICE TRACKING

Let legitimate alerts watch a route

Use Google Flights to track itinerary prices and booking-window insights when available. A price alert detects changes; it does not hold inventory or buy tickets.

03 · AFTER BOOKING

Recheck the identical itinerary

Compare flight numbers, passenger eligibility, departure date, cabin and fare restrictions. Check the booked airline's actual change/refund terms, any fees, seat and baggage consequences, and whether value comes as a credit or cash.

04 · THIRD-PARTY OPTION

JetBack is optional—not CC's booking engine

JetBack advertises ongoing checks for selected airline reservations for US$99.99/year. It primarily supports major U.S. carriers and may seek future-use airline credits. Read its terms and privacy/reticketing powers before personally choosing it.

What CC will not claim: every Tuesday is cheaper; incognito/VPN is guaranteed to defeat price discrimination; booking at 3–4 AM creates savings; all fare drops entitle you to cash back; or a travel provider must issue a credit merely because CC found a lower advertised price.

Understand the rules

A fare credit is different from a passenger-rights refund.

Voluntary price-drop credit

The airline may allow a qualifying ticket to be reissued at a lower price, with the difference issued as a future-use airline credit. The fare rules and issuer's policies control; no universal credit right.

Cancellation or major schedule change

Consumer-protection refund rights involve different events and legal standards. For U.S.-covered flights, an airline cancellation or significant schedule change may create refund rights when the traveler declines alternatives.
